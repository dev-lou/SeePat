The five questions

Five things a store owner should be able to answer on the spot

₱8,000 in sales is not ₱8,000 in profit. Some of it is the goods, some is the day's expenses, some is utang that has not arrived, and some is money already taken home. SeePat closes the gap between money moving and money earned.

The five questions, in the owner's own words, and what SeePat answers instead
The owner asks Magkano talaga ang kinita ko? Profit, not the drawer Sales, minus what the goods cost you, minus the day's expenses — worked out again after every record. Utang still counts as profit, and it says so, in the same sentence, that the cash has not arrived yet.
The owner asks Nasaan ngayon ang puhunan ko? Where your money actually is Cash in the box, stock at what you paid for it, utang owed to you, equipment — and what you still owe suppliers, taken off. It adds up, on one screen.
The owner asks Bakit malakas ang benta pero kulang ang cash? Sales are not cash GCash, Maya, bank transfers and utang are each written down as what they are, so a busy day stops looking like a rich one.
The owner asks Ano ang mabilis maubos? What to buy again What actually sells, what is sitting still, and what to restock — taken from your own records, not from a guess.
The owner asks Ano ang dapat kong gawin bukas? One thing to do first “Collect first.” “Reorder this.” “Watch that.” And why, in plain words, when you want the reason.

The question the notebook never answers

Ask it — by typing or by talking — and SeePat answers with the same figures the rest of the app uses, and shows you how it got them. Nothing is guessed.

Everything you own, minus everything you owe, is your capital. It always adds up, to the last peso, which is what makes the number worth believing.

Boses

Say the sale. Confirm the numbers.

“Sipat, nakabenta ako ng sampung Coke at limang Lucky Me.” SeePat writes up the record, shows you every line, and waits.

Nothing is saved until you agree. That is on purpose: a wrong item is easy to see on the screen, but a wrong quantity or price quietly spoils every answer that comes after it. So the numbers are always yours to confirm.

The part that understands speech runs on your phone. Download it once and it keeps working with no signal, and it costs you nothing each time you speak — which is why voice is in the free plan rather than behind it.

Cash, GCash, Maya, utang

Money comes in four ways. The drawer only sees one.

Utang is the part nobody keeps properly: a sale counted today, paid in three weeks if at all. SeePat keeps track of who owes what, takes part-payments, and keeps the unpaid balance in front of you instead of letting it quietly become profit that never arrived.

Taking money out for the house is not an expense either. It leaves your pocket emptier; it does not make the shop earn less. The two stay in separate lines, which is what makes the profit figure mean something.

  • Utang with a balance

    Part-payments, a warning if you are overpaid, and who to collect from first.

  • GCash, Maya and bank

    Written down as the ways money comes in, and kept apart from the cash in the box.

  • Money you take home

    Kept out of your expenses, so the shop's profit is about the shop.

Supplier receipts

The supplier hands you a receipt. That is a purchase.

Purchases are the records nobody keeps: a long thermal receipt that fades in your pocket, while your stock numbers quietly stop matching what is on the shelf. Photograph it and SeePat reads the lines, matches them to your items, and writes up the purchase as a draft.

You check every line before anything is recorded. The draft says plainly which lines need a look, warns you if the total does not match, and never posts on its own — the same rule as voice: nothing is saved until you agree.

The reader is downloaded once and runs on your phone. It reads with no signal, and it costs nothing per scan — which is why receipt scanning is in the free plan rather than behind it.

  • Photo → draft → confirm
  • Runs on your phone
  • Free, reader included

FAQ

The questions owners ask first

Does SeePat work without internet?

Yes — that is the whole point. Your sales, your utang, your capital and the score are all worked out on the phone itself. The part that understands speech is downloaded once, on Wi-Fi, and after that the app keeps working with no signal at all. The receipt reader downloads once the same way, then reads supplier receipts on the phone. Nothing you record leaves your phone.

Do I need to know accounting, or English?

No. You can talk to it in Taglish — the way a sale is actually said out loud — and it answers in Filipino or English, whichever you pick.

What does SeePat cost?

Everything that works on your phone is free, and stays free: the records, utang, the capital breakdown, the answers, voice, receipt scanning and the SeePat Score. SeePat PRO is ₱99 a month for the parts that need the internet, starting with longer written explanations. You cannot be charged today — payments are not switched on yet.

Where do my records go?

They stay on your phone. You can save a copy to a file and keep it wherever you like — a memory card, your email, another phone. There is no account to make, and nobody else has a copy.

Is the SeePat Score a credit score?

No. It is a score out of 100 for how your shop is doing — profit, cash habits, utang and stock — based on your own records. It is not a credit score, and no lender uses it to decide anything.

How are GCash, Maya and utang handled?

They are recorded as what they are: ways the money comes in. GCash, Maya and bank transfers are kept apart from the cash in your box and from utang, so a busy day stops looking like a rich one, and the cash figure stops going negative.
