The five gaps

Five questions a store owner should be able to answer on the spot

₱8,000 in sales is not ₱8,000 in profit. Some of it is the goods, some is the day's expenses, some is utang that has not arrived, and some is money already taken home. SeePat closes the gap between money moving and money earned.

The five questions, in the owner's own words, and what SeePat answers instead
The owner asks Magkano talaga ang kinita ko? Profit, not the drawer Revenue minus the cost of the goods, minus the day's expenses, recomputed after every record — with utang still counted as profit and explicitly flagged as not yet cash.
The owner asks Nasaan ngayon ang puhunan ko? Where the capital actually sits Cash in the box, stock at what you paid for it, utang owed to you, equipment — and what you still owe suppliers, subtracted. It adds up, on one screen.

The question the notebook never answers

Ask it — typed or spoken — and SeePat answers from the same figures the rest of the app uses, and shows the breakdown that produced the answer. No guessing, no chatbot, no invented number.

Every figure comes with its evidence, and the accounting identity underneath it holds exactly: assets minus liabilities equals capital, minus what you took out, plus everything you have earned.

Cash, GCash, Maya, utang

Money arrives in four ways. The drawer only sees one.

Utang is the part nobody keeps properly: a sale that is counted today and paid in three weeks, if at all. SeePat tracks who owes what, accepts partial payments, and keeps the unpaid balance visible instead of letting it quietly become profit that never arrived.

Taking money out for the house is not an expense either. A withdrawal reduces what you have; it does not reduce what you earned. The two lines stay apart, which is what makes the profit figure mean something.

Utang with a balance

Partial payments, overpayments flagged, and a list of who to collect from first.

Channels, not cash

GCash, Maya and bank transfers recorded as themselves, reconciled in one ledger.

Take-home stays separate

A withdrawal reduces the pocket, not the profit — so the profit stops lying.

FAQ

The questions owners ask first

Does SeePat work without internet?

Yes, that is the point. Recording sales, the ledger, utang, the capital breakdown, the answers and the score are all computed on the phone. The voice model downloads once on Wi-Fi and then keeps working with no signal at all. Nothing you record is uploaded.

Do I need to be good at accounting, or at English?

Neither. You can speak to it in Taglish — the way a sale is actually said — and the app answers in Filipino or English, whichever you choose.

How much does SeePat cost?

The whole local product is free: the ledger, inventory, utang, the capital view, the answers, voice capture and the Sipat Score. SeePat PRO is ₱99 a month for the parts that need a server, beginning with AI narration. Payments are not switched on yet, so nothing is being sold today.

Where does my data go?

Nowhere by default. Your records live in the phone's own storage and you can export a backup file you control. There is no account and no server in the free product.

Is the Sipat Score a credit score?

No. It is a measure of how the shop is doing — profit, cash discipline, utang and stock — scored out of 100 from your own records. It is not a credit bureau score and it is never used to decide a loan.

What about GCash, Maya and utang?

They are recorded as what they are: channels money arrives through, kept apart from cash in the box and from utang. That is why a busy day stops looking like a rich one, and why the cash figure stops going negative.
