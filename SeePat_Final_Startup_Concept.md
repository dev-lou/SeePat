# SEEPAT

## The Financial Copilot for Every Filipino Negosyo

**Brand lockup:** `See` in navy + `Pat` in gold, with the sun mark — *See
Your Business Clearly.* as the English line, `Maliit na Negosyo.
Malaking Bukas.` as the Filipino one.

> **Hindi lang magkano ang benta. Alamin kung magkano talaga ang kita,
> nasaan ang puhunan, at ano ang susunod na dapat gawin.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Executive Concept

**SeePat** is a mobile-first, voice-enabled financial and business
decision copilot designed specifically for Filipino micro and small
entrepreneurs---particularly sari-sari stores, carinderias, market
vendors, home-based sellers, small retailers, food stalls, service
businesses, and other owner-operated enterprises.

It addresses a deceptively simple problem:

> **Many small-business owners know how much money entered the cash box
> today, but not necessarily how much they actually earned.**

Sales, expenses, inventory, customer credit or *utang*, supplier
obligations, and personal withdrawals are frequently tracked
separately---or not tracked systematically at all. This makes questions
such as *"Kumikita ba talaga ako?"*, *"Nasaan ang puhunan ko?"*, *"Bakit
malakas ang benta pero walang natitirang pera?"*, and *"Ano ang dapat
kong bilhin bukas?"* surprisingly difficult to answer.

The opportunity is substantial. 2024 Philippine MSME statistics report
**1,236,908 MSMEs representing 99.63% of business establishments**, with
microenterprises alone accounting for **1,125,476 or 90.66% of
establishments**.

SeePat therefore does not begin with accounting software.

It begins with the way a Filipino entrepreneur actually runs a
*negosyo*.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. The Problem

A typical microentrepreneur might say:

> "₱8,000 ang benta ko ngayon."

But ₱8,000 in sales is **not ₱8,000 in profit**.

Some of that money represents inventory cost. Some may have gone to
operating expenses. Some sales may have been *utang*. Some cash may have
been withdrawn for household expenses. Some capital may now be sitting
in slow-moving inventory.

The entrepreneur sees **cash movement**, but not necessarily the
underlying **financial state of the business**.

This produces five recurring information gaps:

-   **Profit gap:** *Magkano talaga ang kinita ko?*
-   **Capital gap:** *Nasaan ngayon ang puhunan ko?*
-   **Cash-flow gap:** *Bakit mataas ang benta pero kulang ang cash?*
-   **Inventory gap:** *Ano ang mabilis maubos at ano ang hindi
    gumagalaw?*
-   **Decision gap:** *Ano ang dapat kong gawin bukas?*

DTI's MSME digitalization baseline found that many surveyed MSMEs
remained at low levels of digital adoption and that a large majority
reported a need for capacity building, including financial management.

The problem, therefore, is **not simply lack of POS software**.

It is the lack of **accessible business intelligence for entrepreneurs
who are not accountants or data analysts**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. The SeePat Solution

SeePat turns ordinary business activity into understandable financial
intelligence through a five-stage loop:

**RECORD → COMPUTE → UNDERSTAND → PREDICT → DECIDE**

The entrepreneur can record transactions conventionally or simply speak:

> **"Sipat, nakabenta ako ng sampung Coke mismo, limang Lucky Me, at
> tatlong sardinas."**

SeePat converts the statement into structured transaction records.

Or:

> **"Bumili ako ng ₱2,500 na paninda kay Supplier A."**

The system recognizes it as a purchase and updates the relevant
inventory and capital records.

Or:

> **"Si Aling Nena umutang ng dalawang kilong bigas, ₱120."**

SeePat records the transaction as a receivable rather than cash
revenue.

The objective is simple:

> **The entrepreneur should spend less time operating software and more
> time operating the business.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. The Microenterprise Business Twin

The technological heart of SeePat is the **Microenterprise Business
Twin**.

Every verified transaction continuously updates a digital representation
of the negosyo:

**Cash + Inventory + Receivables + Payables + Sales + Expenses +
Capital + Profit**

Suppose the owner asks:

> **"Sipat, nasaan ang ₱20,000 na puhunan ko?"**

Instead of generating generic AI advice, SeePat examines the actual
business records and might answer:

> ₱7,800 ang cash mo ngayon.\
> ₱8,600 ang halaga ng natitirang paninda.\
> ₱2,100 ang nasa utang ng customers.\
> ₱1,500 ang nailabas bilang expenses.
>
> Malaking bahagi ng puhunan mo ay nasa inventory. May ₱2,300 na
> panindang mabagal ang galaw nitong nakaraang dalawang linggo.

That is fundamentally different from asking a generic chatbot for
financial advice.

The AI operates **on the entrepreneur's verified business state**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Deterministic Finance + AI

A critical design principle of SeePat is:

> **AI should not invent the financial numbers.**

SeePat uses deterministic accounting and business formulas for core
calculations.

### Revenue

$$
Revenue = \sum Sales
$$

### Cost of Goods Sold

$$
COGS = Opening\ Inventory + Purchases - Closing\ Inventory
$$

### Gross Profit

$$
Gross\ Profit = Revenue - COGS
$$

### Net Profit

$$
Net\ Profit = Gross\ Profit - Operating\ Expenses
$$

### Gross Margin

$$
Gross\ Margin = \frac{Gross\ Profit}{Revenue} \times 100
$$

### Inventory Turnover

$$
Inventory\ Turnover = \frac{COGS}{Average\ Inventory}
$$

### Receivable Exposure

$$
Receivable\ Exposure = \frac{Outstanding\ Receivables}{Total\ Sales} \times 100
$$

AI sits **above this computation layer**.

Its job is to identify patterns, explain relationships, forecast likely
outcomes, and translate numbers into understandable recommendations.

### Trust Architecture

**Business Records → Financial Engine → Business Twin → Analytics/ML →
AI Explanation → Owner Decision**

Not:

**Business Records → LLM → Financial Answer**

This separation makes SeePat more accurate, auditable, explainable,
and technically defensible.

## 7. Ask My Negosyo

One of SeePat's signature experiences is **Ask My Negosyo**.

Instead of forcing owners to interpret dashboards, they can ask their
business directly:

-   *"Magkano kinita ko ngayong linggo?"*
-   *"Ano ang pinakamalakas kong paninda?"*
-   *"Ano ang hindi ko muna dapat i-restock?"*
-   *"Sino ang may pinakamalaking utang?"*
-   *"Bakit bumaba ang kita ko?"*
-   *"Bakit wala akong cash kahit malakas ang benta?"*
-   *"Nasaan ang puhunan ko?"*
-   *"Kung ganito ang benta ko, magkano kaya ang kikitain ko next
    week?"*

SeePat converts natural-language questions into queries against the
Business Twin and returns concise answers with supporting evidence.

This is **Conversational Business Intelligence**, rather than merely a
chatbot bolted onto a POS.

## 8. Explainable Decision Intelligence

Recommendations should always answer three questions:

1.  **WHAT happened?**
2.  **WHY did it happen?**
3.  **WHAT should the owner consider doing?**

Example:

> **⚠️ Mababa ang cash mo ngayon.**
>
> **Bakit?**\
> ₱4,250 ang naitalang benta nitong linggo, pero ₱1,180 dito ay utang at
> ₱1,620 ng puhunan mo ay nasa additional inventory.
>
> **Napansin namin:**\
> 27.8% ng sales mo ay hindi pa collected.
>
> **Maaaring gawin:**\
> Bago magdagdag ng malaking inventory order, maaari mong unahin ang
> collection ng outstanding customer credit.

The AI therefore does not merely say *"Reduce expenses."*

It explains **why** a recommendation emerged from the owner's own data.

> **Where did my money go?**

## 10. Sipat Score

Sipat Score should **not** be an arbitrary AI-generated score.

It should be an explainable composite indicator based on measurable
dimensions such as:

-   Profitability
-   Cash-flow health
-   Inventory efficiency
-   Receivable exposure
-   Expense control
-   Sales stability

Example:

### Sipat Score: 76/100 --- MAAYOS

> **Malakas:** Profit margin and inventory turnover\
> **Bantayan:** Customer utang\
> **Problema:** 31% of this week's sales remain uncollected\
> **Priority:** Collect receivables before significantly increasing
> inventory.

Every score must be decomposable into its contributing metrics.

## 11. Predictive Intelligence

Once sufficient historical data exists, SeePat moves from explaining
yesterday toward anticipating tomorrow.

It can progressively provide:

-   **Demand forecasting** --- expected demand by product
-   **Sales forecasting** --- expected revenue over the next days or
    weeks
-   **Stockout prediction** --- likelihood that an item will run out
-   **Slow-moving inventory detection** --- capital trapped in products
    unlikely to sell soon
-   **Cash-flow forecasting** --- expected cash position
-   **Receivable-risk indicators** --- customers or balances showing
    delayed-payment patterns

The progression becomes:

**Traditional POS:** What happened?

**Bookkeeping:** What did it mean financially?

**SeePat:** What happened → Why → What may happen next → What should
I consider doing?

## 14. Hyperlocal Financial UX

SeePat should not feel like accounting software translated into
Filipino.

It should feel as though it was **designed around Filipino
microentrepreneurs from the beginning**.

  Conventional Financial Term   SeePat Language
  ----------------------------- --------------------------------------
  Accounts Receivable           **Utang sa Iyo**
  Net Income                    **Tunay na Kita**
  Inventory Turnover            **Gaano Kabilis Maubos ang Paninda**
  Working Capital Allocation    **Nasaan ang Puhunan Ko?**
  Revenue                       **Benta**
  Expenses                      **Gastos**
  Inventory                     **Paninda**
  Profit                        **Kita / Tubo**

Underneath these labels remain rigorous accounting definitions and
formulas.

The interface simplifies the **language**, not the mathematics.

### 14.1 The visual language

SeePat is a **light-interface product**, and that is a functional
decision rather than a stylistic one. A sari-sari owner reads this
screen under a canopy at noon, sometimes through plastic, often with
the brightness turned down to save battery. Dark themes lose that fight,
so the canvas is a light, slightly cool off-white with soft light
bleeding in from the top edge, and every text step is verified against
it.

Against that quiet canvas, exactly **one surface inverts**: the jewel
card that carries the day's real profit or the Sipat Score. It is a deep
navy body with a diagonal sheen, a gold sun glowing in one corner, a
grain overlay and a 1px gold highlight along its top edge. The point is
not decoration. Money is the one thing the owner opens the app to see,
so money gets the only surface on the screen that looks like a physical
object, and everything else stays deliberately flat so nothing competes
with it.

### 14.2 The palette is the flag

The product's colour system is the national flag, and it is load-bearing
rather than decorative. An owner does not need a legend to read navy as
the system, gold as value, and red as attention: they have seen that
combination their whole life, on the flag in the barangay hall and on
every sari-sari store sign painted by the same sign-maker.

-   **Navy — the flag's blue.** All structure: chrome, primary actions,
the active tab, the jewel card. The wordmark spells "See" in it.
-   **Gold — the flag's yellow.** The sun in the mark, the "Pat" in the
    wordmark, the Sipat Score gauge, and the trend line *on navy* — which
    is why the day's chart reads as a sunrise over the ledger. The
    wordmark is deliberately set at 1.2rem bold so gold clears the
    14pt-bold line and only has to reach 3:1 there, instead of being
    darkened into brown to satisfy 4.5:1.
-   **Red — the flag's red.** Negatives, danger and the recording mic.
-   **Emerald — the one deliberate exception.** Green is kept for state,
    never chrome: money coming in, and a confirmed outcome (draft posted,
    payment done, model available). A Philippine bank statement still
    prints credits in green, and "kita" reading as green is a stronger
    signal to an owner than palette purity is. It never brands anything.

Three rules hold the rest of it together:

-   **One accent, one material.** Navy carries the interface and gold is
    reserved for value and premium, so "PRO" reads as a material rather
    than as another shade of blue.
-   **Figures are the interface.** Balance and score are set in a
    geometric display face at 2.9--3.2rem with tabular figures, and the
    heaviest visual weight on any screen is always a number, never a
    label or a container.
-   **Motion explains, it does not decorate.** Each screen has one
    orchestrated entrance; money counts up so the ledger reads as live;
    the trend line draws itself; the mic waveform is flat when idle and
    animated only while listening. Every one of those animations is
    disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

Data visualisation follows the deterministic engine rather than
decorating it: a smoothed area chart for the 7-day sales shape, an
8-week Sipat Score trend that switches from a curve to columns when a
week has no sales (an area chart would have to plunge to zero and invent
a collapse that never happened), and a donut for *Nasaan ang Puhunan
Ko?* where each arc is a component of capital. Charts whose data is
sparse say so instead of drawing a zero.

The mark is the same idea in one glyph: the **Philippine sun around an
eye**, eight gold rays on navy as the flag's sun, and an eye at the
centre because *sipat* means to observe — the product's promise is that
the owner can finally see where the money went. Both meanings have to
survive at 32px, which is why it is one shape and not a lockup.

Accessibility is **audited, not asserted**: `npm run check:contrast`
computes all 65 shipped colour pairs and fails the build if any text pair
drops under 4.5:1 or any meaning-carrying data mark under 3:1. The
current worst case is 4.67:1 for text and 3.19:1 for a chart fill, and
the script records which graphics are decorative and therefore exempt
(chart baselines, gradient washes, glow overlays, and the illustration
itself).

### 14.3 What makes a light interface look expensive

The first build of SeePat was light, correct and *flat*: white cards on a
pale page, each with a single grey border and one flat shadow. Nothing
was broken and nothing looked designed, because a card that is one solid
colour with a stroke around it is a wireframe however polished the type
inside it is.

Five things fix that, and none of them is a pattern:

-   **A fill with a top and a bottom.** Every light card is filled with a
    very shallow vertical gradient (white to `--color-card-deep`), so the
    surface catches light instead of being a rectangle of colour. The
    Sipat Score is still verified against the *foot* of that gradient,
    because that is where dark text has least contrast.
-   **Elevation in three layers, never one.** A 1px contact shadow where
    the card meets the page, a mid layer that models it, and a wide
    ambient pool that suggests height. A single soft shadow is the tell of
    a template; three stacked ones are what make white-on-white read as
    stacked paper.
-   **Edges as alpha hairlines, not grey strokes.** A 6% navy ring tints
    with whatever is underneath it, so the edge belongs to the surface
    rather than being drawn around it. Opaque `#e5eaf3` borders are what
    make an interface look like a form.
-   **A drafting surface under the content.** A 22px micro dot grid at
    barely-visible alpha, *dissolving by mid-page*, sitting behind the
    cards rather than in them. Faded out it reads as the surface the
    interface was measured on; run across a whole canvas it would read as
    graph paper and compete with the data.
-   **Materials used with meaning.** Three tinted variants of the same
    surface — gold for value (the plan you are on), navy for a reading the
    engine produced, sunken for a well *inside* a card — plus a flat
    variant for state cards that bring their own fill (amber for a
    warning, emerald for a confirmed outcome). A card is tinted when it
    *means* something, never to fill space.

The texture question deserves a straight answer, because it is the one
people usually get wrong: **the pattern goes behind the interface, not
inside the cards.** A grid or noise inside a card fill puts a pattern under
the numbers, costs legibility, and dates the work to a specific couple of
years of dribbble. What reads as expensive is *light* — gradient, ring,
shadow, and one accent — with the texture held at the edge of perception
underneath.

### 14.4 Pat, the owl

SeePat has a mascot, and it is one bird. **Pat** is named for the second
half of the wordmark, and it is an owl because *sipat* means to observe —
an owl is the animal that sees in the dark, which is the product's promise
in one silhouette.

The rule that keeps it from becoming a stock illustration is that the
character never changes, only its **wardrobe**: the same body, the same
white face disc, the same navy eye as the brand mark, in every
appearance. What changes is the prop that matches the screen's job —
an **apron and pen** when Pat is keeping the books (Dashboard),
**headphones** when it is listening (Voice), **reading glasses** when it
is answering questions (Ask, and *sipat* again), and a **beanie** when
you are about to go offline (Offline). A mascot that changes shape per
screen is a set of illustrations; one that changes clothes is a
character. On the navy jewel card the body inverts to lit gold, exactly
as the sun in the mark does, but the face is identical in both tones,
because a face that changed with the surface would be a different bird.

**It is drawn with inline SVG and CSS keyframes, not Lottie or Rive**, and
that is a cost decision before it is an aesthetic one. This app precaches
its shell so it works with no signal, and every install pays that weight
in prepaid data: a Lottie player would add roughly 250KB of runtime and
Rive roughly 100KB of WASM — a 20–46% larger install — to put a bird on
the dashboard. Pat costs a few KiB, adds no dependency, makes no network
request, and cannot fail offline.

The illusion of life is **timing, not library**. Every part moves on its
own period — float 4.4s, breathe 3.6s, sway 5.1s, wings 6.2s, two eyelids
at 4.3s and 6.9s, ear tufts 7.3s, a gaze scan at 9.7s, a gauge ring at
26s — so nothing lines up twice and the loop never becomes visible. The
blink lasts about 130ms, the length of a real one, and the two
independent lids occasionally overlap into a double blink. The eyelids
aftermotion never stops, in any mood: an earlier version held them still
when Pat was worried, and a bird whose only obvious motion was disabled
read as a static image.

**Pat never invents a word.** Its mood is derived from the Sipat Score
band or from the real voice pipeline (a ledger error outranks a soft
margin; "working" is tied to the on-device model actually transcribing
rather than to a decorative spinner), and its speech bubble is always a
sentence the deterministic engine already produced and already
translated — a ledger warning first, then a score priority, then a
strength, then an honest "keep recording". That keeps the mascot inside
the same trust boundary the AI card makes visible: there is no second
place in the product where language about the owner's money is written.

It is accessible by construction rather than by luck: the mood is
announced to screen readers in the reader's own language ("Pat —
nag-aalala"), the state is always also spelled out in words, and under
`prefers-reduced-motion` the movement stops but the *pose* does not — a
worried Pat still looks worried with the animation switched off. As
brand artwork the illustration is exempt from contrast thresholds, in the
same way a logotype is; what is *not* exempt is the sentence beside it,
which is ordinary UI text and is audited with everything else.

## 15. Offline-First and Low-Friction Architecture

SeePat should be designed as a **mobile-first Progressive Web App
(PWA) with offline-capable transaction recording**.

A store owner should still be able to record:

-   Benta
-   Gastos
-   Utang
-   Bayad
-   Bili
-   Inventory movements

even with unreliable connectivity.

Transactions synchronize when connectivity returns.

AI-intensive features can operate when online, while deterministic core
business calculations should remain available locally wherever
technically practical.

This prevents internet availability from becoming a prerequisite for
basic business recording.

## 18. Responsible AI and Trust

Because SeePat deals with financial decisions, it should operate
under non-negotiable trust principles:

-   AI never silently modifies financial records.
-   Voice-generated transactions require confirmation when confidence is
    insufficient.
-   Financial calculations come from deterministic formulas.
-   AI recommendations show supporting evidence.
-   Forecasts are presented as estimates, not guarantees.
-   Users can inspect and correct underlying transactions.
-   Sensitive financial information is encrypted and access-controlled.
-   Business data should not automatically be used to train external AI
    models.
-   Critical decisions remain with the entrepreneur.

This establishes a foundation for **trustworthy AI for microenterprise
finance**.

## 20. Validation Strategy

SeePat should be piloted with approximately **30--50 real
microenterprises for 8--12 weeks**.

Validation should measure:

-   Record completeness
-   Time required to record transactions
-   Accuracy of calculated profit
-   Inventory discrepancies
-   Uncollected receivables
-   Stockout frequency
-   Owner understanding of profit versus sales
-   Frequency of AI recommendation adoption
-   User retention
-   Voice transaction accuracy
-   User trust in AI explanations
-   Willingness to pay
-   Perceived usefulness
-   Decision quality

The central validation question is:

> **Can entrepreneurs make measurably better decisions with SeePat
> than with their previous notebook, calculator, spreadsheet, or manual
> method?**

That evidence can turn SeePat from an interesting application into a
defensible startup.
