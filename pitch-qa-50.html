Round 1 — The problem

1Problem
What problem does SeePat solve, in one breath?›

Millions of Filipino owners know today's sales — but not today's profit.

A sari-sari owner can tell you ₱8,000 entered the box, yet not how much she actually earned once inventory cost, expenses, utang sales, and money taken home for the household are counted. SeePat closes that gap.

2Problem
Who exactly is this for?›

Primary: sari-sari stores, carinderias and food stalls, public-market vendors, home-based sellers, and neighborhood micro-retailers — owner-operated businesses run without an accountant.

Secondary (go-to-market): cooperatives, LGUs, livelihood programs, and microfinance institutions that can sponsor cohorts of stores.

3Problem
How big is the market, with sources?›

2024 Philippine MSME statistics (DTI/PSA, via PIA): 1,236,908 MSMEs — 99.63% of all establishments, of which 1,125,476 (90.66%) are microenterprises.

DTI's digitalization baseline adds the gap: 23% of surveyed MSMEs used no ICT tools, 51% used only basic tools, 6% used advanced tools — and 73% reported needing capacity building, especially financial management.

4Problem
What are the “five information gaps”?›

Profit · Capital · Cash-flow · Inventory · Decision.

Magkano talaga ang kinita ko? Nasaan ang puhunan ko? Bakit malakas ang benta pero walang cash? Ano ang mabilis maubos? Ano ang gagawin ko bukas? — every SeePat screen answers one of these five.

5Problem
Why don't notebooks, calculators, and chat messages suffice?›

They fragment the truth: sales in one notebook, utang in another, expenses in memory, GCash history in an app. Nothing connects sales, expenses, inventory, receivables, and cash into one reliable view — so the owner sees cash movement, not the financial state of the business.

6Problem
Why hasn't existing POS software solved this?›

The problem was never just missing POS software. It is the absence of accessible business intelligence for entrepreneurs who are not accountants or data analysts. Incumbents record transactions well; none explains why the numbers moved or where the capital sits in words an owner understands.

7Problem
How do you know owners really can't state their profit?›

Our interview pack (12–15 owners, Dingle and nearby barangays) makes this the core hypothesis — “can she state her profit without counting?” — with a baseline interview and a five-question findings sheet. We state plainly: fieldwork is pending, and the pilot re-asks the same question at exit so the claim becomes a count, not an anecdote.

Round 2 — Solution & product

8Product
What is SeePat in one sentence?›

A mobile-first, voice-enabled financial decision copilot for Filipino microentrepreneurs.

Not accounting software first — it begins with how a negosyo is actually run: speak or type a sale, and get back profit, capital, utang, stock, and what to do next.

9Product
How does it work, end to end?›

RECORD → COMPUTE → UNDERSTAND → PREDICT → DECIDE.

The owner records (voice, text, or form) → deterministic formulas compute the books → the app explains in Filipino or English → restock and collection guidance follows → she decides. A spoken “Sipat, nakabenta ako ng sampung Coke…” becomes a structured draft she confirms — never an auto-posted record.

10Product
What is the “Business Twin”?›

The technological heart of SeePat: a live digital representation of the negosyo — cash + inventory + receivables + payables + sales + expenses + capital + profit — updated by every verified transaction. Ask “Nasaan ang puhunan ko?” and it answers from her own ledger: cash tonight, stock value, who still owes, what was spent.

11Product
What are the core modules?›

Sipat Ledger (sales, purchases, expenses, cash), Inventory (stock, COGS, fast/slow movers, restocking), Utang (receivables and repayments), Voice (speech/text to transactions), Insights (explainable recommendations), Score (business-health indicator), capital views (Nasaan ang Puhunan Ko?), and receipt scanning (photo → draft → confirm).

12Product
How does receipt scanning work?›

Photograph a supplier receipt and the on-device reader drafts the purchase: lines matched to her items, totals checked, mismatches flagged. She reviews every line before anything is recorded — the same confirm-before-save rule as voice. Free in the base plan; only a future cloud second-read for unreadable photos sits behind PRO.

13Product
How does it handle utang?›

Utang is a first-class receivable, not cash revenue: who owes, how much, part-payments with running balances, overpayment warnings, and a “collect first” priority when exposure is high. The clearest roadmap gap — follow-up reminders and shareable statements via Messenger/SMS — is specified and ranked first.

14Product
What is the Sipat Score?›

A versioned, decomposable shop-health indicator out of 100 — six weighted dimensions summing to exactly 1.00 (profitability .25, cash-flow .20, inventory .15, receivables .15, expense control .15, sales stability .10). Same ledger, same score, every point attributable. It is a behavior measure of the shop — never a credit score, never a lending decision.

15Product
What changes for the owner “tomorrow morning”?›

She opens the app and sees: real profit today, cash on hand, who still owes (collect first — ₱4,158.65 in our demo), what is running low (reorder this), and the one thing to do first. That is the decision gap closing — from “malakas ang benta” to “ito ang gagawin ko.”

16Product
What platforms does it run on?›

A mobile-first Progressive Web App: runs in the browser, installs on Android, works offline once cached. No account, nothing sent anywhere — the ledger never leaves her phone. A Kalagayan panel shows live connectivity, cache state, mic availability, and exactly which features work with no signal.

Round 3 — Technology & trust

17Technology
Where does AI sit in the system — exactly?›

Business Records → Financial Engine → Business Twin → Analytics → AI Explanation → Owner Decision. Never Records → LLM → Answer.

Every figure is computed by deterministic formulas from the verified ledger; AI is confined to interpretation, explanation, and prediction. That makes the system accurate, auditable, explainable — and runnable offline at zero marginal cost.

18Technology
How is profit actually computed?›

Perpetual inventory at moving weighted-average cost: cost of goods sold is derived at the moment of each sale, so daily profit is available without counting shelves. Values are exact integers in centavos — the books cannot drift. The accounting identity Assets − Liabilities = Capital Injected − Withdrawals + Cumulative Net Profit is asserted after every transaction by an automated test suite.

19Technology
What are the “two capital questions”?›

Composition — Nasaan ang puhunan ko? Where capital sits right now (cash + paninda + utang owed to her + equipment − supplier debt). Provenance — Saan napunta ang pera ko? How capital was built and consumed since inception. Withdrawals and expenses are flows, never locations — both views reduce to the same identity, so they can never disagree.

20Technology
How does voice capture avoid costly mistakes?›

Three deliberate constraints: (1) constrained vocabulary — the recognizer distinguishes items she actually sells plus numbers, not open transcription; (2) numbers never auto-post — a misheard quantity silently corrupts everything, so amounts always need her tap; (3) drafts, not records — voice output cannot persist until she accepts it. “AI never silently modifies financial records” is a property of the software, not a promise.

21Technology
Which voice engines, and why?›

Google's server recognizer is the default (best Taglish accuracy). The offline fallback is a multilingual Whisper Tiny model (~42MB, MIT-licensed) downloaded once with consent, transcribing fully on-device. The 320MB Filipino Vosk model was evaluated and rejected: ~19% word error on clean speech, ~98% on BABEL, and a CC-BY-NC-SA license that forbids commercial use — and we say so openly in the app.

22Technology
What about Taglish accuracy honestly?›

Free-form Taglish runs ~19–25% word error even on the best engines — which is precisely why we do slot-filling against her known SKU list instead of open transcription, keep voice an accelerator with a typed equivalent for every flow, and never let even a hallucinated sentence reach the ledger unconfirmed.

23Technology
What works with no internet?›

Everything that matters daily: recording, the deterministic engine, the Twin, capital views, deterministic answers, receipt scanning, and downloaded voice. Only two features need a server — AI narration and cloud backup — which is exactly why only those two are sold.

24Technology
How is owner data protected?›

The ledger never leaves her phone — no account, no upload. Backup is an owner-controlled export file (memory card, email, another phone). During the pilot, the team sees figures only when she shows or reads them out. Speech goes to Google only in online mode; the offline voice never sends audio anywhere.

25Technology
How do GCash and Maya fit in?›

Recorded as channels money arrives through, kept apart from drawer cash — so a GCash-heavy day never reads as an empty box, and cash can never nonsensically go negative. Reconciling cash, GCash, Maya, bank, and utang in one shop ledger is one of our differentiators; deep e-wallet reconciliation is honestly listed as not yet built.

26Technology
Is household money separated from store money?›

Yes — and this is a differentiator. Money taken home (kinuha para sa bahay) is recorded as a withdrawal, never as a store expense, so profit stays about the shop. The audit's own recommendation is to make this even more explicit in the UI as “money you took home.”

27Technology
How mature is the prototype, technically?›

Working prototype, pre-MVP: records cash and utang sales, purchases, expenses, payments, capital injections, withdrawals, and stock adjustments end to end, with the accounting identities asserted by an automated test suite (400+ tests). Not yet delivered: accounts, cloud backup, first-run onboarding for non-team owners, and the pilot release.

Round 4 — Business model

28Business
What is the pricing?›

FREE ₱0 for life · PRO ₱99/month or ₱999/year.

FREE is the entire local product — ledger, inventory, utang, capital views, voice, offline model, answers, Score and history. PRO is only the two things that genuinely need a server: AI narration and a cloud copy that survives a lost phone. The rule is enforced in code, so the free tier can never quietly become loss-making.

29Business
Why is giving so much away free sustainable?›

On-device is free at the margin: recognition happens on the phone, the answer layer performs no inference, and there is no per-user server cost. What costs us nothing to run, we charge nothing for — permanently. That is also what lets a ₱99 price exist at all in a market where competitors' free tiers already cover basic bookkeeping.

30Business
What are the unit economics of one PRO store?›

Effective revenue ₱94.27/month (30% annual prepay honestly applied, not ₱99 quoted flat), GCash fee ~₱2.35 (PayMongo 2.23% + VAT), AI narration placeholder ₱7.50 (deliberately high), infrastructure ₱1,450/month past ~150 stores (~3,000 stores per project). Break-even on variable cost is immediate per PRO store — and card payments (3.125% + ₱13.39) would make ₱99 uneconomic, which the pilot must test.

31Business
What does the revenue projection say?›

Generated by arithmetic in the repo, not hand-written: base case reaches ~₱271k annual run-rate in year one and ~₱701k revenue in year two (conservative ~₱194k, optimistic ~₱1.67M). Even the optimistic case is ~0.3% of microenterprises — reached through institutional partners, never claimed as national market share. Stated plainly: ₱99 subscriptions alone are not a salary.

32Business
Why are institutions the primary channel?›

Direct ₱99 subscriptions are a hard market against free competitors — so cooperatives, LGUs, and livelihood programs buying ₱120/store/year cohort licenses are the primary revenue line, with retail PRO as the second. One letter of intent puts 10–20 stores in reach through a partner owners already trust — distribution and revenue in the same signature.

33Business
What happened to the ₱199 NEGOSYO plan?›

Withdrawn from sale and deleted from the plan list — it survives only as a retired receipt name so old invoices still read correctly. Existing holders migrate to PRO without a price rise, never silently down to free. Two plans, one list, nothing that can disagree: FREE and PRO.

34Business
How will owners pay?›

GCash (and Maya/card) via PayMongo, with a simulated checkout in the prototype that states plainly no real payment happens. The pilot measures the payment mix — because if owners pay mostly by card, the fee structure forces a rethink of the ₱99 price point itself.

Round 5 — Competition

35Competition
Who are the competitors, and what do they do well?›

Peddlr (2M+ stores served) — POS, inventory, expenses, ledgers, offline. Packworks (~270k stores, DOST-backed ML) — sari-sari digitization. Lista — simple bookkeeping and receivables. StoreHub/full POS — retail depth. We credit them by name in the concept note — claiming otherwise is the fastest way to lose a judge's confidence.

36Competition
What is genuinely different about SeePat?›

Capital decomposition · grounded answers · explainable score · Taglish voice · offline at ₱0.

No reviewed competitor answers “nasaan ang puhunan ko?”, grounds every answer in the owner's verified ledger, decomposes its score, designs voice around Taglish business speech, or runs its intelligence offline with zero marginal AI cost. Ledgers and offline selling are table stakes — we match them; these five are the reasons to switch.

37Competition
Where is SeePat honestly behind?›

Payments and e-wallet reconciliation, receipt-printer and barcode hardware, multi-branch depth, and any distribution footprint. Deliberate near-term trade-offs, not oversights — which is why our beachhead is microenterprise decision support, not retail infrastructure.

38Competition
Why can't an owner just ask ChatGPT about her business?›

A generic chatbot gives generic advice — possibly fluent, possibly wrong, grounded in nothing. SeePat computes from her verified books: the numbers are hers, the explanation cites them, and the whole thing works offline with no per-question cost. AI that cannot see her ledger cannot tell her where her puhunan is.

39Competition
Does it issue official receipts or handle taxes?›

No — and we state it plainly. SeePat is a management-accounting tool: it helps an owner understand her own business. It is not a BIR-registered POS and does not issue official receipts. That avoids a heavy accreditation program and sets correct expectations — a strength, not a gap.

Round 6 — Pilot & validation

40Pilot
What is the pilot plan?›

Thirty microenterprises in and around Dingle, Iloilo (18 near the ISUFST campus + 12 at a public market as a contrast site) use SeePat free for 8–12 weeks: baseline interviews, in-store onboarding (slower than 20 minutes is a product defect), weekly support visits, exit interviews — including stores that stopped using it.

41Pilot
What exactly is measured?›

Records per trading day, time to record one transaction (the number that decides everything), profit accuracy via manual recomputation on 3 stores, inventory discrepancy on top-10 items, uncollected receivables vs baseline, profit-vs-sales understanding, voice first-attempt accuracy, trust in explanations, willingness to pay asked twice (before and after real use), and retention at weeks 8 and 12.

42Pilot
What would make the pilot a failure?›

Stated in advance so results can't be reinterpreted: fewer than half the stores still recording at week 8 · recording slower than the notebook · profit that doesn't reconcile on manual check · no store able to name a price at exit (then retail subscription dies and the model falls back to institutional). A pilot that can fail is a pilot a panel can trust.

43Pilot
What is the current validation status — honestly?›

Working prototype with deterministic-engine test coverage; interview pack and findings structure ready; pilot planned for 30 stores. Field interviews not yet conducted — every placeholder figure is marked as such, because a filled-in table nobody collected would be worse than an empty one. The committee asked for interviews and findings; we show the instrument, not invented results.

44Pilot
How much does the pilot cost?›

Within the already-filed ₱150,000 budget: ₱15,000 user research and validation, ₱20,000 pilot deployment and onboarding, plus transport. No new funding requested for this phase. Stores are never paid to participate — payment would make the willingness-to-pay measurement meaningless.

Round 7 — Risks & limitations

45Risks
What is the single biggest risk?›

Time to record a transaction.

A bookkeeping app slower than the notebook is not used, however correct it is. That is why the pilot times 3 transactions per store at weeks 2 and 8 — and why voice must become the primary path, not an accelerator, if typing loses to paper.

46Risks
What other risks keep you up at night?›

Voice accuracy on real noisy-store Taglish · distribution against incumbents with millions of stores · card-payment economics breaking ₱99 · data loss during a pilot (logged with date and recovery, never smoothed over) · scope creep into POS hardware instead of decision support. Each has a named mitigation in the concept note and audit.

47Risks
What is deliberately NOT built, and why?›

Forecasting, benchmarking, staff accounts, and bulk reports need a server nobody has paid for yet — parking them was the right call. E-wallet reconciliation, printer/barcode hardware, and multi-branch depth are explicit near-term trade-offs. The honesty rule holds: no screen gates a door onto a room that was never built.
