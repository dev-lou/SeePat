# Revenue projection — SeePat / SIPAT LABS

**Why this document exists.** The PSC XI screening committee's feedback on the Business Model
criterion was one sentence long, and it is the only one that asked for a number:

> *"The project offers free access, 99 PRO, and 199 NEGOSYO subscriptions, along with institutional
> licenses. The 150,000 budget adds up. However, the relatively low subscription prices mean the
> business will need a large number of paying users to generate sufficient revenue. The team should
> provide a revenue projection based on realistic adoption rates."* — DICT screening committee,
> PSC XI evaluation and feedback form

This is that projection. It is **generated by arithmetic in the repository**, not written by hand:
`node tools/revenue-model.mjs` rewrites [`revenue-projection.csv`](revenue-projection.csv) (24 months
× 3 scenarios) and prints the tables below. Anyone can re-run it and check every figure.

**Two notes on what this is not.** It is not a market-share claim — adoption is expressed as
penetration of the stores we have actually onboarded, never as a slice of the country's 1.1 million
microenterprises, because no credible plan captures a national market in 24 months. And it is not a
commitment: the pilot exists to replace every assumption below with a measurement.

---

## 1. The two plans and what they cost to serve

The pricing rule the product enforces in code (`src/billing.ts`) is *on-device is free, server is
paid*, and it survives the retirement of the ₱199 tier:

| Plan | Price | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **FREE** | ₱0 | The whole local product: ledger, inventory, *utang*, capital views, voice capture, the offline voice model, deterministic answers, the Score and its week-by-week history. **No account, no server, no marginal cost to us.** |
| **PRO** | ₱99/month, or ₱999/year (~2 months free) | The two things that genuinely need a server: **AI narration** of the owner's own figures, and **a safe copy of the records that survives a lost phone**. |

The free tier costs SeePat nothing per user to serve: recognition happens on the phone, and the
answer layer performs no inference. That is what allows a freemium price of ₱99 in a market where the
competitors' free tiers already cover basic bookkeeping (Peddlr states it has served more than two
million Filipino stores; Packworks' network is reported at over 270,000).

## 2. Assumptions, and the basis for each

| Assumption | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PRO monthly / annual | ₱99 / ₱999 | Published in the app's own pricing screen |
| Share taking the annual plan | 30% | Price-sensitive market; prepay is the norm. **Pilot measures this.** |
| Effective monthly revenue per paying store | **₱94.27** | The annual discount applied honestly — quoting ₱99 would overstate the top line by ~5% |
| Payment fee | 2.4976% of collected | PayMongo's published GCash rate (2.23% + 12% VAT). The **card** rate (3.125% + ₱13.39) would make a ₱99 charge uneconomic, which the pilot must also test |
| AI narration cost | ₱0.25 per question, 100 questions/month, 30% of paying stores | **A deliberately high placeholder**, so the margin cannot be flattered by an unmeasured assumption. It replaces the note's earlier figure, which modelled *cloud* speech recognition the product does not use |
| Infrastructure | ₱1,450/month once PRO passes ~150 stores | Supabase's $25/month Pro plan (≈₱1,450 at ₱58/USD), needed for backups and to stop free projects pausing |
| Stores per paid infrastructure project | ~3,000 | 8 GB of database at ~2.5 MB of encrypted backup per store per year |
| Institutional licence | ₱120 per covered store per year | A cooperative or LGU buying a cohort, not a consumer price |
| Pilot | 30 stores, February 2027, free | The milestone the note already commits to |

## 4. Unit economics of one paying store

| Item | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Effective revenue | ₱94.27 | 30% annual prepay at ₱999 |
| Payment fee | ₱2.35 | 2.4976% GCash + VAT |
| AI narration | ₱7.50 | 30% using it, 100 questions at ₱0.25 (placeholder, deliberately high) |
| Infrastructure | ₱0.48 | ₱1,450/month covering ~3,000 stores |
| **Contribution** | **₱83.94** | **89% gross margin** |

**Break-even on infrastructure:** the free tier holds about 150 stores' encrypted backups at no cost,
and the first Supabase invoice is covered by **16 paying stores**. From the first subscriber onward,
each store covers its own variable cost. Server cost never decides whether the business works.

## 5. The answer to the committee, stated plainly

The objection is correct, and the projection says so rather than arguing with it:

- **₱99 subscriptions alone are not a salary.** In the base case the subscription line reaches
  ₱58,828/month at M24 — before any salary, and only after several hundred paying stores. Retail
  subscriptions in this market are a *validation* signal and a modest revenue line, not the business.
- **Institutional licences are the revenue line, not a later add-on.** In the base case they overtake
  subscription revenue while covering 3,900 stores — because a cooperative pays once for a cohort
  and brings its own distribution. This is the same conclusion the concept note reached
  independently when it made B2B2C the primary channel.
- **The margin is high enough to survive being wrong.** At 89–93%, the unit economics still work if
  the AI cost placeholder is several times too low, or if penetration is half of the base case.
- **What is deliberately not claimed:** no revenue is projected before the pilot reports. The first
  twelve months are validation; a projection that showed profit during the pilot would be a
  projection nobody should believe.

## 6. What would falsify this

Stated because a model that cannot be wrong is not a model:

1. **PRO penetration below ~4% of onboarded stores after the pilot.** Then the retail tier is a
   retention feature rather than a revenue line, and the institutional channel carries everything.
2. **Institutional procurement taking longer than 12 months.** Government and cooperative
   purchasing has its own calendar; if the first cohort licence slips past M12, the base case moves
   down to the conservative one.
3. **Payment mix going to cards.** At 3.125% + ₱13.39, a card-dominant mix costs ~₱16.48 on a ₱99
   charge — the fee becomes the largest cost line, and the fix is prepaid periods rather than
   monthly billing.
4. **AI narration usage far above 30% of stores at full questions.** Even at four times the modelled
   cost the contribution stays above ₱70, but this is the assumption with the least evidence behind
   it and it is metered in the product for exactly that reason.
